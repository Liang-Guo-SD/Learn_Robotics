Top 3 only

The numbers are invisible. Twelve of fourteen route digits are painted over by their own color discs because the badge group precedes the disc in document order. The map's primary navigation claim ("numbers = sequence, follow the numbers") is void on the map body, and no text-collision audit can catch an occlusion. One-line z-order fix restores the artifact's central function.
The grid is not a taxonomy of one population. The vertical axis classifies methods (how behavior is produced); the horizontal axis classifies components (where they sit in a running system). Infrastructure items get fake capability-sources (ros2_control → "Engineering Base"), methods get fake stack layers (ACT → "Learning · Models"), column F becomes a dumping ground (PyTorch, HF Hub, OpenCV/PCL, W&B, BT, LLM planning), column C is empty below R1, and 25 of 49 cells are empty with no rule for which empties matter — including the missing perception column that visibly misfiles three items. Mutual exclusivity is unattainable here, and the "secondary cell dot" patches a wound that will keep bleeding.
Half the route is drawn on the grid. 880 of 1794 mm (49.1%) of the route polyline is pixel-coincident with gridlines, plus ~31 mm of literal back-and-forth retracing with mismatched dash phase, plus amber/grey running 1.4 mm apart for 120 mm. At print the second navigation channel (the grey dashes) reads as border decoration, not as a path. The three internal review rounds audited text collisions and missed that the map's two navigation channels are both illegible.

Attack on the specification

Section 1's premise — "every item occupies exactly one cell" of a method-axis × component-axis grid — is a category error, not a design choice. A grid is MECE only if both axes partition the same population. Here they partition two different populations, so exclusivity fails by construction and exhaustiveness fails by omission (perception has no column because perception is neither a capability-source nor, in the author's runtime view, a "layer"). The observable symptoms are exactly the predicted ones: 25/49 empty cells, column F as catch-all, G hiding four roles (inference runtime, calibration, evaluation, packaging), R6 mixing three criteria (world models + closed-access VLAs + whole-body research) so that π0.5 and GR00T N1.7 — architecturally the same kind of object — land in different rows, and R1/R7 defined by prerequisite-ness and weight-mutability rather than by any capability source. The "secondary cell dot" cannot repair this: a dot on a marker cannot name a different cell, and the map proves it by leaving SmolVLA's dot unexplained while ROS 2 (which spans R1–R7) and MuJoCo (R2/R3/R4) get at most one named secondary.

A better specification, same ink: keep the horizontal axis as the runtime stack including perception, and replace the vertical axis with one criterion that applies to every item — "where the decision is made" (hand-coded → explicit-model → learned policy → frozen foundation model → frozen model + external harness) — while moving "prerequisite vs tool vs method vs dataset" into the glyph family the map already half-owns (★/☆/◇/▲/■). For a beginner audience an even better form is two panels: a layered stack diagram (what runs on the robot) and a method genealogy (how behaviors are learned), joined by the numbered route. A 7×7 grid buys a learner nothing that this loses, and it is what forces the author into the "empty waters" rhetoric — declaring B×R2–R3 "specialist depth" is a blind spot dressed as a market gap, because B×R2 (1 kHz servo loops, PREEMPT_RT, trajectory interpolation) is precisely what a robotics software interview covers.
